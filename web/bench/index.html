Method how it was scored

  • The draw. Games drawn at random from the Final 2026 regular-season games, stratified by month, after excluding the games inspected while the model was developed. A game that fails to replay is reported, never replaced.
  • What is scored. Every row Bend simulated whose scorebook state matches MLB's after the play. The final row, where the game is already decided, is excluded. A play the encoding refuses leaves every later row of that game unscored: –.
  • The model. Each side bats at the previous season's league rates against the other side's pitching through the day before the game: the team's, shrunk toward the league by 2,000 batters faced, blended with the probable starter's (shrunk toward his team's by 600) by the share of batters he is expected to face, with the previous season's home/away factors. In the simulated games, an out in play with a runner on first and fewer than two outs is a double play 21% of the time; otherwise a runner on third scores 61%, on second takes third 49%, on first takes second 21%; a single scores a runner from second 61% and sends one from first to third 35%; a double scores a runner from first 38%. Regular-season extra innings start with a runner on second.
  • Chosen elsewhere, scored once. Every setting above was chosen on the other 2,315 Final 2026 games (tune.py), where the model's Brier is 0.1554 against MLB's 0.1571 on the same plays (Δ −0.0017, 95% CI −0.0028 to −0.0006; in-sample for those choices). These games were then scored once. This is the second model scored on them: the first (team batting and pitching to date, a simpler simulator) scored 0.1653 against MLB's 0.1670 on the 93 games it completed; this one scores 0.1662 against 0.1671 on those same 93.
  • The metrics. All pooled over plays. Brier is the mean squared error of the home win probability against the result (0 or 1); log loss clips forecasts to [0.0001, 0.9999]; skill is 1 − Brier divided by the Brier of always forecasting the observed home win rate; right side of 50% counts a forecast of exactly 50% as half.
  • The intervals. 10,000 bootstrap resamples of whole games (seed 2026), so plays from one game move together. A clear win or loss means the 95% interval excludes zero; otherwise no clear difference. The games column adds an exact two-sided sign test on per-game Brier.
  • Market prices are a reference. Each venue's bid/ask midpoint from minute history, read up to 60 s after the play ended (Kalshi's home and away markets normalised to sum to 100); no fee adjustment, books wider than 10 points dropped.
  • A benchmark, not a claim of superiority. MLB's win probability is a production model with far more information than this proof of concept uses.

Reproduce same seed, same games

python3 bench.py --draw 100 --seed 2026   # fixtures-bench/pks.json
python3 bench.py --web                    # replay, score, write web/bench/data.json
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v

Fixtures download on the first run into fixtures-bench/cache/ and are reused after that. The model's settings come from the development games: python3 tune.py fetch, then python3 tune.py advancement (the simulator's runner advancement) and python3 tune.py variants (the rate model against the alternatives it was chosen over).
